# Prospective Management Operating Model

> **Prospective—not a claim of past formal data-team management.** This is how I would operate as a people manager. My evidence base is technical leadership of five IRS contractors for two years, Army leadership, Math Department Chair, Test Prep Coordinator, and Head TA experience.

## 1:1s

- Hold a recurring private meeting with each report, normally every other week and adjusted to need.
- Let the employee lead a durable agenda: outcomes, blockers, relationships, development, and feedback.
- Separate status collection from the 1:1; use team operating artifacts for routine status.
- Track commitments and revisit them, while keeping sensitive notes appropriately limited.
- Ask regularly what I should start, stop, or continue doing.

**Signals:** useful commitments closed, blockers aged, growth opportunities distributed, and themes acted on without exposing confidences.

## Hiring

- Define the outcomes, competencies, and decision rights before opening the role.
- Use a structured rubric and consistent core questions.
- Include a realistic work sample tied to the role; avoid trivia that does not predict performance.
- Require interviewers to record independent evidence before debriefing.
- Calibrate the panel on “must have now” versus “can learn.”
- Close candidates respectfully and document the decision.

**Boundary:** I would follow HR/legal requirements and the company’s approved process. I am not claiming prior hiring-manager experience for a data/BI team.

## Feedback and performance

- Give specific, timely feedback using observed behavior, impact, and the needed next action.
- Reinforce effective behavior, not only gaps.
- Agree on measurable expectations and support, then follow up.
- Address persistent performance issues early and involve HR according to policy.
- Use recurring evidence rather than recency or personality.

**Example foundation:** At the IRS I directed five contractors’ work and enforced quality control, but formal ratings, compensation, or corrective action are not verified responsibilities.

## Prioritization

- Maintain one visible backlog with owner, user, expected outcome, risk, effort, dependencies, and evidence of urgency.
- Protect regulatory, safety, production-continuity, and data-correctness work first.
- Rank remaining work by impact, confidence, effort, and strategic fit.
- Reserve capacity for reliability and debt instead of treating them as invisible work.
- Revisit priorities with stakeholders on a fixed cadence and record tradeoffs.
- Limit work in progress and define what must stop when an urgent item enters.

**Verified foundation:** weekly/monthly stakeholder touchpoints, standing Yield meetings, daily triage, Jira Scrum, and delivered manufacturing reporting.

## Incidents

- Establish severity, user impact, incident lead, communications owner, and technical owners.
- Stabilize service first; preserve evidence and avoid speculative changes.
- Communicate facts, uncertainty, actions, and next update time.
- Keep a timestamped decision log.
- After recovery, run a blameless review covering detection, response, contributing conditions, and owned corrective actions.
- Track prevention work to completion and test the runbook.

**Boundary:** This model is prospective. Verified experience includes MES Phase I hypercare, 15-minute log monitoring, daily triage, production-data diagnosis, build alerts, and uptime monitoring—not a documented 24×7 rotation or formal incident-command role.

## Team cadence

- Weekly: delivery/reliability review and risk escalation.
- Biweekly: individual 1:1s and backlog refinement.
- Monthly: stakeholder outcome review, architecture/quality trends, and development check.
- Quarterly: goals, capacity, succession risk, skills plan, and operating-model retrospective.

The cadence would be adapted to the organization rather than imposed unchanged.
