# Mock Interview 3: Leadership and Management

## Format

45 minutes. Distinguish demonstrated leadership from the prospective operating model.

## Question 1: Tell me about leading engineers

**Model answer:** “At the IRS I was technical lead and overseer for five contractor developers across my two-year tenure. I directed work, kept the team on task, and ran quality control. The broader program delivered 13 automations associated with more than $12 million in savings, while we also used dev/test/prod environments and shared components. I am careful not to call that formal people management: hiring, compensation, and formal ratings are not verified parts of the role.”

**Rubric (0–3):**

- **3:** Defines team, duration, actions, broader result, and formal-management boundary.
- **2:** Clear leadership but vague on mechanisms or result.
- **1:** Relies on title rather than behavior.
- **0:** Claims formal management or sole ownership of all savings.

## Question 2: How do you respond when a stakeholder resists your conclusion?

**Model answer:** “I move the conversation from authority to evidence: process grain, actual records, and a known-correct output. On a global OEE dashboard, I raised that quality always appeared as 100% and that AutoCaster data collapsed all equipment into one unit. Corrected quality figures were then verified. My evidence does not document a heated confrontation or a named person pushing back, so I present this as constructive challenge rather than an interpersonal-conflict story.”

**Rubric:** Strong answers show curiosity, evidence, shared validation, and honest limits. Deduct for dramatizing conflict.

## Question 3: How would you run 1:1s and feedback?

**Model answer with prospective label:** “This is my prospective management model, not a claim that I have formally managed a data team. I would hold recurring employee-led 1:1s focused on outcomes, blockers, relationships, and growth rather than status collection. Feedback would be timely and specific: observed behavior, impact, and next action, followed by agreed support and a check-in. My foundation is technical direction and quality control for contractors plus Army, department-chair, teacher-coordination, and Head TA leadership.”

**Rubric:** Full credit requires the prospective label, a practical cadence, and evidence-based feedback.

## Question 4: How would you hire?

**Model answer with prospective label:** “Prospectively, I would define outcomes and competencies first, use consistent questions and a role-relevant work sample, have interviewers record evidence independently, and debrief against a rubric. I would separate true day-one requirements from learnable skills and follow HR and legal processes. I am not claiming prior data-team hiring-manager experience.”

**Rubric:** Look for structure, fairness, job relevance, independent evidence, and explicit boundary.

## Question 5: How do you prioritize competing requests?

**Model answer:** “I make work and tradeoffs visible: user outcome, risk, effort, dependency, confidence, and urgency. Safety, regulatory, production-continuity, and correctness risks come first; then I rank value while reserving capacity for reliability. In manufacturing I maintained weekly or monthly stakeholder touchpoints, standing Yield meetings, and daily triage. Prospectively as a manager, I would use one backlog, limit work in progress, and explicitly identify what stops when urgent work enters.”

**Rubric:** Strong answers combine principles, cadence, transparent tradeoffs, and reliability capacity.

## Question 6: Are you ready to be a formal manager?

**Model answer with honest boundary:** “I have two years of verified technical leadership over five contractor developers, Army leadership of about 30 combat medics plus a doctor and physician assistant, and leadership as Math Department Chair, Test Prep Coordinator, and Head TA. I have not formally managed a data or BI team for three to five years, so roles requiring that exact tenure are a stretch. I am ready to apply an explicit operating model and learn the company’s formal hiring, performance, and compensation processes, but I would not erase that distinction.”

**Rubric:** Full credit requires confidence without inflating formal-management tenure.

## Question 7: What is your incident-leadership approach?

**Model answer with prospective label:** “My formal incident operating model is prospective. I would establish severity, impact, incident lead, communications owner, technical owners, and the next update time; stabilize first, preserve evidence, keep a decision log, and follow with a blameless review and tracked actions. My verified foundation is MES hypercare, 15-minute log monitoring, daily triage, build alerts, and uptime monitoring—not a 24×7 rotation or formal incident-command role.”

**Rubric:** Strong answers distinguish response structure from past claims and include communication, evidence, recovery, and learning.
